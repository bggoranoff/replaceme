# Minigame research

What well-loved games do for simple minigames, and what we borrow. Our minigames use arrows and mouse only, must work for any job, and the actor must be able to learn a hidden optimum from feedback.

## By mechanic

| Our mechanic | Reference | How it works | What we take |
|---|---|---|---|
| craft | Papa's Freezeria | A pour meter sweeps; release on the moving star for 100%. The cup has a fill line: over or under loses points. Blend needs the right time under the mixer, and holding the booster too long overheats it. Toppings are spread by moving the mouse left and right; off-balance or clumped scores lower. | Pour, steep, stir |
| craft | Potion Craft | No recipe list. You grind, add, stir (speed matters) and pump bellows for heat while a bottle moves across a map toward an effect. Dead centre = tier III, partial overlap = tier I. | Show the result live instead of numbers |
| craft | Coffee Talk, Venba | Three ingredients where order matters, and you only learn after serving (5 retries). Venba's recipe book is smudged; you fill the gaps by trying. | Learning by trial, a natural fit for the actor |
| track | Stardew Valley fishing | Hold the mouse to raise the bar, release to let it fall; it bounces at the bottom. A catch meter fills while the fish is in the zone and drains outside it. A perfect catch (never slipping) bumps quality silver → gold. | Heat, pressure, steaming: anything "keep it in range" |
| track | Mario Golf 3-click | Press to start the meter, press to set power, press in the contact zone for accuracy. Too early or too late gives a slice or fade. | Two-step "set amount, then execute" |
| trace | Okami brush | Forgiving: an oval counts as a circle, and a bad stroke only costs ink. Valid start and end points glow. | Lenient path scoring |
| trace | PowerWash Simulator | Dirt disappears instantly under the spray, with a "ding" when a part reaches 100%. The threshold is really about 95–98%; the last specks auto-clear. | Cleaning, painting, polishing |
| deliver / sort | Diner Dash | Hearts over each customer drain while they wait. Repeating the same action earns a chain bonus. Matching seat colours gives combos; holding customers back for a combo is a real strategy. | Learnable who-to-serve-first choices |
| deliver / sort | Papa's Pizzeria | Each ticket is scored on waiting time, accuracy (count, spacing, placement), doneness against the ticket and cut evenness. 80%+ earns a star, and the result screen shows a breakdown. | Score card format |
| converse | Moonlighter | Set a price; the customer shows one of 4 emoji: Cheap (<88%), Perfect (88–104%), Expensive (105–120%), Overpriced (>120%). Players find the price by trial and error. | Hidden optimum plus readable reactions; ideal for the actor |
| converse | Good Pizza Great Pizza | Orders are vague or quirky ("smelly toppings", "pepperoni pizza without pepperoni"). Interpret them, or ask and risk annoying the customer. | Conversation becomes a choice of what to make |
| converse | Potionomics | Haggling: cards raise the customer's interest, and the deal fails when their patience runs out. | Too complex; skip |

## Rules that held across games

1. **Pass is easy, perfect is hard** (Rhythm Heaven, Stardew perfect catch). Show bronze / silver / gold tiers and a perfect bonus.
2. **Feedback is instant and physical.** A ding at 100% (PowerWash), Mama reacting to the grade (Cooking Mama), a sound for every placement (Unpacking uses 14,000 foley files).
3. **Readable in one second** (WarioWare). One verb, one goal, controls you grasp at a glance.

## Strongest idea for us

Moonlighter-style reactions: four emoji tiers let a human and the actor feel their way to a hidden best value (dose, price, pressure) without ever seeing a number.
